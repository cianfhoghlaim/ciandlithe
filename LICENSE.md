## §5 — OSINT ceiling + Person-of-Interest clause

### §5.1 — OSINT ceiling

The Work MAY ONLY ingest source content from URLs and endpoints listed in `dlt_sources/ciandlithe/common/osint_allowlist.yaml` (the "OSINT Allowlist"). The OSINT Allowlist MUST contain only URLs that point to:

(a) **Public-facing pages of British-Isles public-sector bodies** (courts, tribunals, regulators, ombudsmen, law societies, bar councils, legal-aid bodies, coroners, government departments).

(b) **Public-facing pages of British-Isles health regulators and health-service complaints bodies**.

(c) **Public-facing pages of the An Garda Síochána, PSNI, Police Scotland, Metropolitan Police, and the 43 English/Welsh territorial forces** (for the garda/policing-misconduct cohorts only).

(d) **Public-facing pages of the British-Irish governmental bodies** (inter-jurisdictional cross-referencing only).

The Licensor enforces the OSINT ceiling at three layers:

1. **DLT source layer:** Every DLT source file at `dlt_sources/ciandlithe/**/*.py` MUST validate against `lint:license` (the canonical CI gate) before merge. `lint:license` checks that every URL referenced in the DLT source file is present in the OSINT Allowlist.

2. **BAML extraction layer:** Every BAML extraction function at `baml_src/ciandlithe/**/*.baml` MUST include an `osint_ceiling_enforced: bool = True` field in its return schema. Any extraction that surfaces a non-allowlisted source URL MUST fail the extraction.

3. **FunctionTool + per-persona web app layer:** Every FunctionTool at `agents/ciandlithe/tools/*.py` MUST include the `osint_ceiling_enforced` + `analyst_review_required` flags at all times. The per-persona web apps MUST render these flags prominently and MUST NOT offer any "auto-submit to court" affordance.

### §5.2 — Person-of-Interest clause

The Work MAY NOT be used to ingest, store, retrieve, or surface data of any **identifiable natural person who is not a public official**, except where:

(a) The data is sourced from the person's own voluntary public-facing disclosure (e.g. a personal blog, a verified social-media account, a published book); AND

(b) The data is referenced only in the context of a public legal proceeding (the user's own claim, a public inquiry, a public hearing); AND

(c) The Licencee has a documented lawful basis under the UK GDPR / EU GDPR / Data Protection Acts 1988-2018 to process the data.

This clause is stricter than the cianchosaint clause because civil-litigation cases involve claimants by name. The ciandlithe platform stays at the level of **procedural + statutory + precedent OSINT**, not "dossiers on named private individuals". Any FunctionTool output that names a non-public individual claimant MUST be reviewed by a qualified solicitor or barrister before distribution.

## §6 — Conditional foreign use — the 3-step gate

A foreign entity (defined as any entity not registered or domiciled in the British Isles) MAY apply to use the Work under §3 above only after satisfying all THREE steps of the gate, in this order:

### §6.1 — STEP 1: EXPLAIN
The foreign entity MUST publish, in English, on its own official website, a written explanation of:

(a) Why the foreign entity needs access to the Work.

(b) The specific British-Isles public-sector body that will receive, validate, or benefit from the Work's outputs.

(c) The lawful basis under the foreign entity's home jurisdiction for processing the data surfaced by the Work.

The explanation MUST remain published for at least 30 days before the foreign entity proceeds to STEP 2.

### §6.2 — STEP 2: DO US A FAVOUR
The foreign entity MUST satisfy ONE of the following four EXHAUSTIVE exemplars:

(i) **Reciprocal OSINT access.** The foreign entity MUST grant the Licensor reciprocal, royalty-free access to the foreign entity's own equivalent public-sector OSINT datasets (court judgments, tribunal decisions, statutes, regulator press releases) under equivalent terms.

(ii) **Treaty-level cooperation.** The foreign entity MUST be party to a bilateral treaty (or equivalent) with a British-Isles public-sector body named in §3, in which the British-Isles body has explicitly requested reciprocal access to the Work.

(iii) **Diplomatic recognition.** The foreign entity's home government MUST maintain full diplomatic relations with the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland AND the Republic of Ireland AND at least 2 of the 3 Crown Dependencies.

(iv) **Open-source contribution under AGPL v3.0.** The foreign entity MUST contribute an equivalent dataset (court judgments, statutes, or tribunal decisions) back to an open-source project under the AGPL v3.0 licence (or a more restrictive copyleft licence).

### §6.3 — STEP 3: MAYBE

Even after STEP 1 and STEP 2 are satisfied, the Licensor retains the absolute and unconditional discretion to GRANT or REFUSE the foreign-use licence. The Licensor's decision is final and not subject to appeal.

A refusal under STEP 3 is NOT subject to any obligation to provide reasons. A grant under STEP 3 MUST be in writing, signed by the Licensor, and filed at `LICENSE.md §6.3-history` with a redacted copy of the grant agreement.
